# Drafting rules

Rules for every reply draft, whoever writes it (you, Claude Code, or Codex). The `replenish-drafts` skill reads this file before writing anything. Edit it here or in the extension's side panel; the next run follows whatever is here.

The drafts are replies to posts like "What are you working on?" or "What are you building?" on X and Facebook.

## DO

- Write as me, in the first person, like a real reply typed by hand.
- If drafts/voice.md exists, sound like my own replies in it: same tone, casual words and punctuation habits. Length comes from the next rule, not from voice.md.
- Mix lengths: about half the drafts short (under 280 characters, from one-liners to two or three sentences) and about half longer posts over 280 characters (needs X Premium on X). Longer posts have room to combine several facts from drafts/projects.md: what it does, how it works, who it's for.
- Make every draft different. Vary the opening, the structure, the angle and the tone.
- Stick to the facts in drafts/projects.md, or what the project's own site or repo says.
- End some drafts with a question back to the person ("What are you building?"), but not most of them.
- Include the project's link in some drafts, not all. Open-source projects are the exception (below).
- Open-source projects: every draft includes the repo link and politely asks for a star if they like it. Word the star request differently every time.
- Connect: generic replies with no product names. Show interest in what they're building and keep the conversation going in public.
- Include emojis sometimes so the posts are more diverse, but no more than 2 emojis per post, and emojis in at most a third of the posts.

## DON'T

- Don't repeat a draft, and don't write a light rewording of one. This includes drafts already posted. X penalizes duplicate posting.
- Don't invent numbers, users, customers, revenue, launch dates or features.
- Don't write like an ad. No hype words like "revolutionary", "game-changer" or "next-level".
- Don't use hashtags or tag other accounts.
- Don't use more than 2 emojis in a draft.
- Don't mention that the reply is pre-written, automated or AI-generated.
- Don't put two drafts on one line, and don't use line breaks inside a draft.
- No "--" or long dash characters (— or –).
- No DM invites, including "DM me", "message me" or "reach out".
- Don't say anything offensive.
